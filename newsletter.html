Improving gameplay and mechanical depth

It has been a massive day of progress for DiscordPHP-MTGPocket, focusing heavily on making the game more accessible and technically robust. I have been working to ensure that new players can jump right in without feeling lost, so I am happy to share that we have merged several features to help with that. Specifically, PR #30 and #33 have brought a new "How to play" quick start guide and the ability to play practice games against a bot into the project. You can even use rental decks to practice against the bot now, which should make getting started much smoother.

Strengthening the engine

Beyond the user experience, I spent a significant amount of time under the hood refining how the engine handles the complexities of the game. To ensure the rules engine is as accurate as possible, I merged a large series of updates, including PR #31 and #34, to better support intricate mechanics like damage prevention, double-faced cards, Sagas, and various "look and reveal" templates. This ensures that when these cards appear in a match, the logic holds up.

I am also continuing to bridge the gaps in our card coverage and deck construction. I've been working through the claude/card-coverage-gaps-sc84tj branch to address these issues, which included a fix in PR #32 to add basic lands to certain short rental decks. To keep the project's foundation professional, I also integrated new PHP tooling via PR #35 and pushed some much-needed updates to the DiscordPHP.org and valgorithms.com websites.

Sunday, October 4, 2026 · Permalink

Expanding mechanics and gameplay modes

It has been a massive day of progress for the DiscordPHP-MTGPocket engine. My main focus was on deepening how the system understands the complexities of Magic: The Gathering. I have successfully merged a significant wave of updates that improve our data coverage, bringing in support for MTGJSON and ensuring we are ready for the future with PHP 8.5 compatibility. This means the engine is becoming much more robust when handling tricky elements like triggered and loyalty abilities, tokens, equipment, and specific mechanical nuances like modal spells or painlands.

New ways to play and manage collections

Beyond just understanding the cards, I have been working hard to make the actual experience of playing more immersive and functional. I have merged several major gameplay systems, including a new /menu panel that allows you to interact using buttons and forms, and a move-by-move match logging system to keep track of your games. I am also very excited to share that a full booster draft mode has been developed, complete with Swiss rounds and card retention.

I have also focused on making your collections more useful outside of Discord. I've implemented features that allow you to export your decks and collections directly to MTG Arena, Tabletop Simulator, and Frogtown. While much of this is now part of the main branch, I am still spending time on the claude/card-coverage-gaps-sc84tj branch to bridge the remaining gaps in how the engine reads specific card text and mechanics. We are getting closer to a truly seamless simulation experience.

Monday, September 28, 2026 · Permalink

Major updates to DiscordPHP core and ecosystem releases

It has been a massive day of progress for the DiscordPHP core. I have pushed out three new releases—v10.64.0, v10.64.1, and v10.65.0—which bring some foundational changes to how the library operates. The biggest shift involves a major refactor of our namespaces to align them with their corresponding API routes, as seen in discordphp/discordphp#1507. This reorganization, alongside the implementation of REST operations and updates to the Factory class, makes the entire library more intuitive and better structured for the long term. DiscordPHP now covers every route.

We also managed to squeeze in several important quality-of-life improvements and safety checks. I've merged updates to improve autocomplete callbacks, added validation for embed field lengths, and implemented new regression tests for invites to ensure everything stays stable as we grow.

Ecosystem growth and maintenance

Beyond the core library, I spent a significant amount of time tending to our wider ecosystem and documentation. The DiscordPHP.org website has been updated with new class diagrams and route maps to help you navigate the library more easily. I also worked on the DiscordPHP-Bridge to move the live announcer over to the Twitch connector, and I released v8.3.0 for the DiscordPHP-Voice extension, which now includes support for a voice session repository.

A large portion of my day was also dedicated to reviewing community contributions. I went through many pull requests, merging several helpful ones while closing others that were either outdated or caused fatal errors. All pending issues and PRs were either closed or merged. It is a constant balancing act to ensure that every piece of code added to the project meets our standards and provides clear benefits to everyone using it.
